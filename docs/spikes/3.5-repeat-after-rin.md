# Task 3.5 measurement: can a just-woken Thai speaker repeat Rin's English sentences, and does mumbling get through?

**Device:** Xiaomi 14T, Android 15 / HyperOS 2.0 · **Build:** debug (Rin soft dev clips, VRoid sample model) · **Game:** `mission/RepeatGame.kt` (rules, matcher, pool), `mission/RepeatAfterRinMission.kt`, `mission/AndroidSpeech.kt` (Vosk, voice), `alarm/ring/RepeatBoard.kt` · **Lab:** `debug/RepeatLabActivity.kt`, `tools/stt/make-protocol.py`, `RepeatRescoreTest`
**Why:** D17 (ADR 0001) makes three games with Rin the way an alarm stops; this is the third, and the only one that uses the mic. S3 found free speech too hard for this user (77%) but a short on-screen grammar reliable (48/48). This game is in between: one known sentence, so two numbers matter. Does the real sentence pass, and does anything else (a mumble, another sentence, half of it) not pass?

## Decisions (2026-09-29, the user picked every recommended option except the voice)
- **Rin's voice:** ElevenLabs "Rin soft" on the free plan (the user's choice over Kokoro). 30 clips made with `tools/voice/make-repeat-clips.py` (`[warmly]`, `eleven_v3`, about 1,100 credits) plus mouth tracks. They are **debug-only and git-ignored** (the free plan has no commercial licence). Release builds show the sentence to read, with a reading pause, until Phase 4's paid month.
- **Mic flow:** the mic opens by itself once Rin finishes, for up to 8 s. The card shows a red mic that swells with the voice, plus a bar that drains; Android shows its own dot. "Hear again" repeats her line.
- **Mic permission:** asked when the user picks the game in the editor (like the camera for QR), plus a Diagnostics row (a note, not a warning). Onboarding is unchanged. Without it, Rin picks leaves this game out.
- **Sentences:** the 30 in `app/src/main/assets/repeat/sentences.json`, approved as drafted and frozen before any clip or recording.

## The game as built (before any data)
- **Let's play**, then three sentences drawn from the pool by the ring's seed, never the same one twice in a game. For each one, Rin says it while the sentence shows on the card; then the mic opens.
- **Two spoken tries** per sentence. A try ends at Vosk's end of an utterance that has words in it, or after 8 s. A miss shows "Almost. Once more?" (or "I didn't hear you") for 1.2 s, then Rin says it again. After the second miss the sentence becomes **word chips**, shuffled (never already in order), to tap in order. A wrong chip shakes and is counted. The chips always finish the game, so an accent or a mumble can't trap anyone (plan).
- **Can't talk right now** (on the card from the start): the ring switches to one of the other games, picked the way Rin picks (logged `MISSION_SWITCHED`). If the mic or the model fails, the game carries on with chips only (`mic=failed:<why>` in the log). It never falls back to a plain Dismiss.
- **Tone:** quiet (≤ 0.15, vibration off) while Rin speaks and during feedback. **Paused, with no vibration,** while the mic is open (`RingState.hush` → RingService, logged `TONE_PAUSED/RESUMED reason=mic`). Once the game starts, the usual rules apply: 30 s without progress brings the tone back. Progress means starting the game, a try with words heard, or a right chip.
- **Rin's voice** plays on the alarm stream, because the media volume may be 0 at wake-up. She nods on each sentence done. A miss gets no sulk: an unheard word is as likely the mic's fault as the user's.
- **Recognition:** Vosk 0.3.75, `vosk-model-small-en-us-0.15` (fetched by Gradle and checked by SHA-256, so it's the same model as S3). It's copied once into device-protected storage, so the game works before the first unlock. The grammar is the sentence's words (contractions in both forms), **plus 83 common decoy words** (none sounding like a pool word, checked by a test), plus `[unk]`. All 107 sentence words, the long forms of the contractions, and the 83 decoys are in the model's vocabulary (checked on the PC with Vosk's `find_word`). Nothing is saved: audio exists only in memory during a try.
- **Judging a try (`MatchRules`, the numbers to tune):** heard words at or above `minConf` count. Contractions are written out on both sides (I'm = I am, let's = let us, don't = do not). The try passes if the longest in-order run of the sentence's words among them reaches `minCoverage` of the sentence (rounded up). **Starting values: minConf 0.6, minCoverage 0.75, decoys on.**
- **Log:** `MISSION_PASSED type=speech … game=repeat sentences=R07,R21,R03 done=3/3 voice=v tap=t listens=n wrongTaps=w hearAgain=h lagMsP50=… seed=s tries=R07.1:6/6ok,R21.1:3/6,R21.2:0/6,R21.t,…`. For each try: the sentence, the try number, and the words heard in order out of the sentence's word count. `R21.t` means passed by chips.
- **Cost:** +41 MB model and ~10 MB native library per ABI. APK 121 MB release / 141 MB debug, with 3 ABIs (arm64, armv7, x86_64 for CI; JNA's mips/armeabi and Vosk's x86 are filtered out). The model also takes ~68 MB on disk once unpacked.

## Smoke rings before any data (not counted anywhere)
Two rings on 2026-09-29 checked the game end to end on the 14T. What they changed:
- **Ring 1 (18:21):** the first sentence missed both spoken tries, and the chips were clipped. The chip rows were centred in a box too short for them, so the top row ("getting", "bed", "now") was out of sight and the sentence couldn't be finished (stopped with the emergency hold). Now the rows start from the top, the area scrolls, the chips are smaller, and the card is 320 dp tall. Also found: a ring that ends without a pass logged nothing about the game. It now logs `MISSION_UNFINISHED` with the summary (all games), and debug builds log what Vosk heard on each try (`RinSpeech`).
- **Ring 2 (18:28):** passed in 50 s, R15 and R27 by voice and R07 by chips. R07 "I am ready to start the day" was heard as `oh are ready two start they day`, then `are ready two start the day`. Two flaws in the rules, fixed before any data:
  1. **"two" was a decoy**, and it took the sentence's "to". "know" (for "no") was the same trap. Both are dropped, and a test now keeps every decoy from sounding like a pool word.
  2. **"I am" said as "I'm"** had no place in the grammar. Contractions now count in both forms.
- **Ring 3 (18:41):** passed in 55 s, with R28 and R05 by voice (R05 on its second try) and R11 by chips. Rin's voice was clear (the tester). R11 "My bed can wait until tonight" was heard as `my bed:0.51 can with into tonight`, then just `my`. The tester said the whole sentence both times. The second try listened 2.3 s, and its last token ended 0.18 s before the result, so it wasn't cut short: Vosk matched the rest to `[unk]`. Decoys taking accent-softened words ("wait until" → "with into", "last" → "like") and correct words just under minConf 0.6 (0.51, 0.55) are exactly what the dev grid decides, so nothing was tuned by feel. The `[unk]` count per try is now logged (`unk=`) and written in the lab results, so a miss can be told apart from a cut-off.

## Protocol (frozen before any data)
**Part 1: recorded tries (the rules).** `tools/stt/make-protocol.py` wrote `app/src/debug/assets/repeat-lab/{dev,heldout}.json`, and both are committed with this file. Each set has 40 tries:
- **24 "say"** (the target sentence): 12 with the phone in hand, 8 lying down with a sleepy voice, and 4 with the phone on the bedside about 1 m away.
- **16 negatives**, which must not pass: 6 **mumble** (sleepy sounds, no words), 6 **other** (the pool sentence that shares the most words with the target), and 4 **partial** (only the first half of the target).
- **Dev uses R01–R12; held-out uses R13–R30.** So the rules are also checked on sentences they weren't tuned on.

1. **Recording.** The tester records each set once in `RepeatLabActivity` (`--es set dev|heldout`): 6 s per try, 9 s for "far". The WAVs stay on the phone and in a git-ignored folder on the PC. The replay results (words and confidences only) are committed.
2. **Replay** runs on the phone, through the game's own `VoskDecoder` (`--es replay <set> --es label <name> --ez decoys true|false`), once with decoys and once without. `RepeatRescoreTest` then re-judges both on the PC for minConf ∈ {0, .3, .5, .6, .7, .8, .9} × minCoverage ∈ {.5, .6, .7, .75, .8, .9, 1}.
3. **Tuning rule (pre-registered), dev only:** among the settings with **zero false accepts** on the 16 dev negatives, take the one that accepts the most "say" tries. Ties go to the stricter setting: higher coverage first, then higher confidence, then decoys on. That setting becomes `MatchRules()` and is committed **before the held-out set is recorded**.
4. **Held-out bars (scored once, with the frozen rules):**
   - **"Say" accepted per try ≥ 80%** (≥ 20 of 24). Reported by condition, plus the chance of passing within 2 tries.
   - **False accepts ≤ 1 of 16** negatives. Each is reported by kind.

**Part 2: rings (the game).** Same as 3.3/3.4:
1. **Setup:** a debug alarm from the PC (`DebugAlarmReceiver --es cmd add --ei sec <45–120> --es mission speech`). The tester lies in bed, screen off, and stops it as on a real morning. Snooze or the emergency hold counts as a fail. "Can't talk right now" also counts as a fail for this game: the switch works, but the game wasn't played.
2. **Dev: 5 rings.** After each, one question: the pause before the mic opens and the 8 s window felt *too short / fine / too long*. If 3 or more of 5 say *too short*, `listenMs` grows by 2 s; if 3 or more say *too long*, it shrinks by 2 s. Feedback times stay. Then freeze.
3. **Held-out: 10 rings**, at least 3 with the lights off. **Pass bar: ≥ 9 of 10 end in MISSION_PASSED within 120 s of RING_START** (the phase bar). Reported, not scored: sentences passed by voice versus by chips, tries, hear-agains, and lag.
4. **Slips:** as before. A trial the tester flags before any data is opened is excluded unopened and replaced, with the deviation written here first. A held-out result that misses the bar goes to the tester with options; nothing is retuned silently.

**Known limits (not measured here):** one speaker, one phone. Noise from a fan or a partner isn't tested. The tone is paused while listening, so it can't interfere, by design. The chip fallback means the game proves the user can read, not that they can speak; the voice-pass rate in Part 2 shows how often speaking actually carries it. Release builds have no voice until Phase 4. Vosk on the live mic was untested in S3; Part 2 is its first real test.

## Dev results (2026-09-29, R01–R12, recorded 18:50–19:02 by file times)
All 40 tries were recorded in one sitting. Peaks were 9–100% (d13, "far", was the quietest at 9%). Replayed on the phone, with results in `docs/spikes/3.5/` (words and confidences only; the WAVs stay git-ignored under `tools/stt/recordings/`).

| Replay | Decoding | Pick (frozen rule) | "say" accepted | False accepts | hand / lying / far |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 (`dev-decoys-*.jsonl`) | try ends at the first utterance | no decoys, 0.6, 0.6 | 19/24 (79%) | 0/16 | 12/12 · 4/8 · 3/4 |
| v2 | meant to collect across pauses; bug, identical to v1 | (same) | 19/24 | 0/16 | same |
| **v3 (`dev-v3-decoys-*.jsonl`)** | **collects across pauses** | **no decoys, minConf 0.6, minCoverage 0.6** | **20/24 (83%)** | **0/16** | **12/12 · 4/8 · 4/4** |

The starting rules (0.6 / 0.75 / decoys on) get 13/24 on v3. **Decoys hurt:** the best setting with decoys gets 16/24 with no false accepts. `[unk]` alone rejected all 16 negatives: mumbles came out empty or as a single low word, and other sentences matched 0–2 words. Confidence makes no difference between 0 and 0.6 without decoys. The thin margin is the partial tries: first halves reach exactly 50% of a sentence (d03, d34 3/6; d40 4/8), and the bar is 60%, so a coverage of 0.5 would accept them (1–2 false accepts in the grid).

The 4 misses under the pick are all lying down, sleepy: `lot to do today` and `to do today` (R12, twice, with "I have a" lost), `deep breath` (R09), `quick` (R06). Vosk lost most of the sentence, so no threshold can fix these; the chips carry them.

**Deviations from the protocol, both before any held-out data:**
1. **How a try ends was changed after seeing dev v1.** The protocol pre-registered only the `MatchRules` grid. But d04 ("The sun is up, and so am I", far) showed a pause at the comma closing Vosk's utterance, and the try then ended with "and so am I" unheard (a full decode on the PC had both halves). The decoder now collects words across utterances. It ends a try as soon as the sentence passes, or 1 s after an utterance with words if no new speech has started, or at the 8 s limit (`VoskDecoder`, tested in `VoskDecoderTest`). The dev set was replayed again, and the same frozen selection rule was applied to the new replay. Replay feeds the whole try without early stopping, which gives the same verdict as live for every rule, because a match only grows with more words.
2. **v2 was a bug of mine:** the "is it enough yet" check finalised the decoder, so v2 decoded exactly like v1. It was found because d04 didn't change, and fixed before v3. The test above covers it.

**Frozen for held-out: `MatchRules(minConf = 0.6, minCoverage = 0.6, decoys = false)`**, with the decoding above and `RepeatRules` unchanged (2 tries, 8 s). Committed before the held-out set is recorded.

## Held-out results (2026-09-29, R13–R30, recorded 19:16–19:25, scored once)
Replayed once on the phone with the frozen rules and decoding (`docs/spikes/3.5/heldout-frozen.jsonl`).

| Bar | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "say" accepted per try ≥ 80% (≥ 20/24) | **18/24 = 75%** (95% CI 55–88%). Hand 11/12, lying 4/8, far 3/4 | **Missed** |
| False accepts ≤ 1/16 | **0/16** (6 mumble, 6 other, 4 partial) | **Passed** |

The 6 misses: h03 hand R22 `weather outside`; h12 lying R16 `wash now`; h15 lying R14 `yesterday`; h17 lying R21 `getting now`; h24 lying R13 `legs our`; h39 far R13 `legs stretch our`. In each, Vosk kept at most 2–3 of 5–7 words, and 4 of the 6 put part of the sentence in `[unk]`. R13 "Let's stretch our arms and legs" missed both times.

**Post-hoc, for context only (not used to pick anything):** no threshold on this data does better without false accepts. Coverage 0.5 accepts the same 18 plus 3 negatives, and 0.7 or more accepts fewer. The limit is the recognizer on sleepy speech, not the rules. Lying down was 4/8 in both sets.

**What it means for the game:** two spoken tries, then the chips. If tries were independent, a sentence would pass by voice within 2 tries about 94% of the time overall, but only about 75% lying down, and tries by the same sleepy speaker are unlikely to be independent. The game still always finishes, because the chips carry the rest. Part 2 (10 rings) measures whether it finishes within 2 minutes, and how much of it is done by voice.

**Decision (the tester, 2026-09-29 19:28):** record the miss as a limit, change nothing, and go on to Part 2. The options put to them were: a bigger Vosk model (lgraph, about +90 MB, with a new held-out set), easier sentences (new clips and new recordings), or pausing the game until Phase 4.
