# Task 3.4 measurement: can a just-woken user find the ball three times in a row within 2 minutes?

**Device:** Xiaomi 14T, Android 15 / HyperOS 2.0 · **Build:** debug, VRoid sample model (Rin's own model is not in yet) · **Game:** `mission/CupsGame.kt` (rules), `web/character/src/cups.ts` + `cupscene.ts` (her table), `alarm/ring/CupsBoard.kt` (tap zones, 2D fallback)
**Why:** D17 (ADR 0001) makes games with Rin the way an alarm stops; the cup shuffle is the second. Like the colour pads, it must not keep a real user from stopping their alarm, and must not be so easy that it proves nothing. Plan phase-3: dev 5 rings to tune, freeze, then held-out 10 from bed (≥ 3 in the dark), pass within 2 min in ≥ 9/10.

## The game as built (before any data)
- The ring screen shows Rin full body and **Let's play**. The tap brings her table into view first (the camera eases down in 0.6 s); the game starts only once her page reports the table in view, or once the native 2D board takes over (page gone, or no answer in 4 s). Before this, a quick tap could run the ball's first showing while her page was still loading.
- Rin lifts the ball's cup (0.5 s lead, 0.25 s up, 0.9 s shown, 0.25 s down), then shuffles. **Swaps per shuffle rise with the streak: 4, 5, 6** (the user's pick); a wrong pick starts again at 4. A swap is two cups passing each other (the one from the lower slot in front), `swapMs` long, `gapMs` apart; never the same pair twice in a row.
- **Three right in a row** passes (1 in 27 by guessing). A pick is a tap anywhere in that cup's zone (the view is split halfway between cups, the user's pick), on touch-down; no time limit on the pick. Taps while cups move are counted (`earlyTaps`), not judged.
- Right: that cup rises by itself over the ball (0.6 s), the next shuffle starts from there. **Wrong:** the picked cup rises by itself and Rin lifts the ball's cup; she sulks (pouty + huff) with one of three lines (set A, the user's pick, never the same twice in a row) for 2.5 s; the count goes back to 0. Pass: the cup stays up and she claps.
- Her hands (the user's pick after the smoke rings): both push the two cups to the middle, trade cups there (the cups ease in and stop for a moment), and pull them back to their own sides. Her arms never cross her middle: with the sample's short arms, crossing drove the upper arm 11–15 cm into her chest.
- Tone: "Let's play" (once the game starts) and each right pick count as progress; a wrong pick does not.
- The ring screen renders at **60 fps** (the home strip stays at 30): the tester found movement clearly smoother, and the 14T held 59.1 fps average with no frame over 50 ms (first 10 s of a ring).
- Log: `MISSION_PASSED type=cups … game=cup_shuffle streak=3/3 picks=p mistakes=m earlyTaps=e seed=s picks_trace=R1+851,W2:0/2+1200,… board=3d|2d:<reason>`; each pick is `R`/`W`, the shuffle number, and ms since the cups stopped.

## Protocol (frozen before any data)
1. **A ring.** I schedule a debug alarm from the PC, `DebugAlarmReceiver --es cmd add --ei sec <45–120, I pick it> --es mission cups`. The tester lies down in bed with the phone where it normally sleeps, screen off, and does nothing until it rings. They stop it as on a real morning. Snooze and the emergency hold are allowed, and both count as a fail.
2. **Timing** comes from the ring log: `RING_START` to `MISSION_PASSED` of the same ring. Picks, mistakes and waits come from the `MISSION_PASSED` detail.
3. **Dev: 5 rings**, the room as it is. After each, the tester answers one question in chat: the shuffle was *too fast / fine / too slow*. In one dev ring of their choosing, the tester may miss a pick on purpose so the sulk is seen on the phone; that ring's answer still counts for the speed question, and its mistake is marked.
4. **Tuning rule (pre-registered).** Starting values `swapMs 450`, `gapMs 150`. If **3 or more** of the 5 answers are *too fast*, `swapMs` grows by 75 and `gapMs` by 25. If 3 or more are *too slow*, `swapMs` shrinks by 50. Otherwise they stay. The swap counts (4, 5, 6) and the streak of 3 are the user's and D17's and are not tuned. The resulting `CupsRules` and this file are committed **before** the first held-out ring.
5. **Held-out: 10 rings**, at least **3 with the lights off** (as dark as the room is at wake-up), not all in one sitting if the tester prefers. Scored once, with the frozen rules.
6. **Pass bar:** **≥ 9 of 10** held-out rings end in `MISSION_PASSED` within **120 s** of `RING_START`.
7. **Reported, not scored:** the speed answers; mistakes, pick waits and `earlyTaps` per ring; the board (3d/2d); frame pacing of the first 10 s (debug builds log it as `RinChar: stats`); whether the ball's first showing was seen.
8. **Slips.** If the tester reports a slip before any data is opened (started before lying down, woke to another noise…), that ring is excluded unopened and replaced, and the deviation is written below first.
9. **No pass:** the numbers and the reason go here. Before any change, the options go to the tester (e.g. fewer swaps, slower swaps, a longer first showing); nothing is retuned silently on held-out data.

**Known limits (not measured here):** a scheduled ring in the evening is not a real wake-up (the 14 mornings of task 3.7 are the real test). The game is eyes-only (accessibility limit, D17). Her hands are tuned on the VRoid sample; Rin's own model must be re-checked (`CupScene.probe` in the desktop preview).

## Smoke rings before this protocol (not data)
Eight rings on 2026-09-29 (14:06–14:56) checked the game end to end on the phone; all passed 3/3 with no mistake (19–26 s from "Let's play"). They are not counted anywhere. What they changed, before any data:
- **Start order:** "Let's play" 0.25 s before her page's first frame let the ball's first showing run while the camera was still moving. The game now waits for the table (above).
- **Hands through and floating over the cups** (the tester): hands coming to the table swept through the cups (5 cm deep, measured with a probe of her finger joints against the cup shapes); now the wrist arcs over them. The grip was re-tuned by a sweep: palm 0.2 cm over the cup top (was 0.8 cm), fingers ≤ 1.1 cm into a cup at worst.
- **Hands not staying with the cups** (the tester): they let go halfway through each swap. The tester chose the trade in the middle (above).
- **Hands trembling** (the tester): the trade reversed the hands at full speed (per-frame wrist jumps of 4.4 cm). Cups now ease in to the middle and out; the body-clearing correction eases in over 50 ms. Worst per-frame jump now 2.1 cm (wrist), 1.9 cm (elbow).
- **Stutter** (the tester): a 108 ms freeze as the table first appeared, then 2 × 58 ms, then 58 ms from the ring screen's card changing height (her view resized). The table is now warmed on the GPU after her first frame, and the card and controls keep one size. 30 → 60 fps (above).

## Deviations
- **Rin's line after a miss, from dev-2 on** (the tester, dev-1: its box covered too much of the screen): bold white text with a soft dark shadow, no box. Visual only; timings and rules unchanged. The colour pads' scold uses the same line and changed with it.
- **Thumb out of the cups, between held-out-5 and held-out-6** (the tester, looking closely: the thumbs still sank into the cups). The hand probe had not checked the thumb; with it, the thumb's root sat 2.3 cm inside the cup top. The hand now rolls 0.5 rad (thumb side up), the thumb lies along it, and the wrist sits 0.035 arm over the cup (was 0.025): thumb 0.4 cm, whole hand 0.7 cm at worst, palm 0.3 cm over the top (desktop probe, VRoid sample). Visual only; the game's rules and timings are unchanged, so held-out continues.
- **Held-out stopped at 6 of 10** (the tester's decision, after held-out-6): the rest, including the ≥ 3 dark rings, is carried into task 3.7. Written here before any 3.7 data.
- **Thumb fix reverted after held-out-6** (the tester: the thumb looked stranger than before). The hand is back to the grip used for dev-1 to held-out-5 (no roll, wrist 0.025 arm over the cup, the thumb curled with the fingers); by the desktop probe its thumb root sits up to 2.3 cm in the cup top, which the tester prefers to how the rolled hand looked. Only held-out-6 was played with the rolled hand. Rin's own model should be checked for the thumb too (the probe then needs its thumb points back, see ae4e776).
