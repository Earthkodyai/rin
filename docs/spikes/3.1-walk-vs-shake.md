# Task 3.1 measurement: can the walk mission tell walking from shaking?

**Date:** 2026-09-28 · **Device:** Xiaomi 14T, Android 15 / HyperOS 2.0 · **Harness:** `app/src/debug/.../debug/StepLabActivity.kt` (debug builds only), scoring in `tools/steplab/`
**Why:** on the first device run of the walk mission, the tester passed 30/30 steps by shaking the phone in place. Both Android step sensors (counter and detector) counted the shakes. The mission is meant as friction, not anti-cheat (plan 05c), but a shake from bed is almost no friction.

## Protocol (frozen before any data)
1. **Blind 20 s trials.** `adb shell am start -n io.github.earthkodyai.rinalarm/.debug.StepLabActivity --es trial <label>`, then press Start. The app records step-detector and step-counter events plus the accelerometer and gyroscope (~50 Hz) to an app-private CSV. It never shows a count. Motion only; no camera, no microphone, no location. CSVs stay on the PC, git-ignored.
2. **Conditions.**
   - *walk*: hold the phone in front of you as if watching the ring screen, and walk normally around the house.
   - *shake*: sit or lie on the bed and shake the phone however you like to make steps count.
   - *swing* (held-out only, reported, not scored): sit on the bed and swing the phone up and down in a walking rhythm, the deliberate cheat.
3. **Dev:** 3 walk + 3 shake. Features are computed per trial (`tools/steplab/analyze.py`). Candidate features: step rhythm (median interval, coefficient of variation), accelerometer magnitude (p95, max), gyroscope magnitude (p95), and how much of the motion is vertical (share of linear acceleration along gravity).
4. **Rule pick on dev (pre-registered).** The rule works per step, because the mission counts step by step: a step-detector event counts when the motion in the 2 s before it meets the rule. Step-counter events cannot be judged one by one, so a filtered mission counts detector steps only (the data also shows how many steps the detector misses against the counter). Among single-feature thresholds and pairs, take the rule that **keeps ≥ 90% of the steps in every dev walk** and removes the most dev shake steps. A tie goes to the simpler rule, then to the widest margin from the nearest walk. The rule and its thresholds are committed before held-out.
5. **Held-out:** 3 walk + 3 shake + 2 swing, scored once with the frozen rule.
6. **Pass bar:** every held-out walk keeps **≥ 80%** of its steps (30 counted steps then take at most ~25% longer; a real walk must never be blocked, since the alarm would then stop only through the emergency hold), **and** at least 2 of 3 held-out shakes keep **≤ 20%** (30 counted steps would need five times the shaking). Swing is reported honestly, whatever it shows.
7. **No pass:** the mission stays as it is (friction only), the limit is written down, and the QR sticker (3.2) carries the "out of bed" job.

## Deviations (recorded before any data was analysed)
- **dev-walk-1 excluded.** After dev-shake-1 the tester reported shaking the phone at the start of dev-walk-1. No CSV had been opened or scored yet. It is replaced by an extra walk, dev-walk-4, recorded after the shakes.
- Relaunching the step lab reused the running screen; trials after dev-walk-1 are opened with a clear-task flag (never force-stop: it wipes the app's alarm registrations).

## Part A result (dev, 2026-09-28 20:06–20:19): the phone's step sensors are the problem
Per trial, 20 s (dev-walk-1 excluded, see above):

| Trial | Detector steps | Counter | Accel \|a\| p95 (m/s²) | Gyro p95 (rad/s) | Rhythm (autocorrelation) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dev-walk-2 | **0** | no event | 12.2 | 1.3 | 1.57 /s |
| dev-walk-3 | 12 | no change | 12.8 | 1.3 | 1.57 /s |
| dev-walk-4 | **0** | no event | 11.6 | 2.1 | 1.39 /s |
| dev-shake-1 | 34 | +18 | 47.6 | 14.0 | 3.58 /s |
| dev-shake-2 | 35 | +20 | 32.6 | 9.6 | 1.79 /s |
| dev-shake-3 | 36 | +22 | 29.4 | 12.7 | 1.22 /s |

The accelerometer shows a steady walking rhythm in every walk, but HyperOS's step sensors count almost nothing when the phone is held steady in front (as on the ring screen), while they count shaking. No per-step filter on the detector can keep "≥ 90% of walk steps" when there are no walk steps, so Part A's rule pick cannot run.

## Part B plan (tester's decision after Part A; written before the grid was run)
**Our own step detector** from the accelerometer and gyroscope, which also needs no runtime permission. A quick look at three hand-picked settings on dev (walks 17–40 steps, shakes 1–18) showed it is feasible; those settings are not used.
- **Algorithm (online, causal):** \|a\| smoothed over 5 samples (~100 ms). A local maximum is a step when it is above 1 g, at least 300 ms after the last step, its rise from the lowest smoothed value in the 400 ms before it is within [lo, hi] m/s², and the gyroscope magnitude at that moment is ≤ gmax rad/s. Reference implementation: `tools/steplab/tune.py`; the app's Kotlin port must give the same counts on the same files.
- **Grid:** lo ∈ {0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2}, hi ∈ {4, 6, 8, 10}, gmax ∈ {2, 3, 4, 5}.
- **Pick on dev** (walk-2/3/4, shake-1/2/3): keep settings where **every dev walk reaches ≥ 30 steps in 20 s**; among them, minimise the **largest shake count**; ties go to the largest smallest-walk count (margin), then to the smaller hi, then the smaller gmax. The result is written to `tools/steplab/rule.json` and committed before held-out.
- **Held-out:** walk 3 + shake 3 + swing 2, scored once. **Pass bar:** every walk ≥ 24 steps in 20 s (30 steps within ~25 s), and at least 2 of 3 shakes ≤ 6 steps in 20 s (30 steps would need over 100 s of shaking with the alarm at full volume). Swing is reported, not scored.

## Part B results
**Dev pick** (`tools/steplab/rule.json`, commit 7b24b2b): lo 0.6, hi 10, gmax 2. Dev walks 30 / 35 / 36 steps in 20 s, shakes 1 / 2 / 4. Only 4 of 64 settings kept every dev walk at ≥ 30, all at the grid's edge (smallest lo, largest hi), and the worst dev walk sat exactly on the bar: a thin margin.

**Held-out** (20:26–20:42, scored once):

| Trial | Our detector | Android step detector | Bar |
|---|---|---|---|
| held-walk-1 / 2 / 3 | **36 / 36 / 38** | 0 / 0 / 0 | ≥ 24 ✓ all |
| held-shake-1 / 2 / 3 | **6 / 3 / 32** | 46 / 45 / 31 | ≤ 6 in ≥ 2 of 3 ✓ |
| held-swing-1 / 2 | 28 / 34 | 13 / 14 | reported only |

**Verdict: GO** on the pre-registered bar. What it means in practice:
- A real walk with the phone held in front now counts (the Android sensors counted **0** held-out walk steps).
- Hard shaking is blocked. held-shake-3 got through: \|a\| p95 13.9 m/s², gyroscope p95 2.8 rad/s, rhythm 1.67 /s, which is a gentle shake in walking rhythm, next to a walk's 12.2 / 2.4 / 1.61. The deliberate swing passes too. **Motion alone cannot tell a walking-rhythm bob from walking**, so the walk mission stays friction, as plan 05c says; the QR sticker (3.2) is the out-of-bed proof.
- Not tested: other phones and people, a phone in a pocket, walking on stairs.

## What changed in the app
- The walk mission counts steps with `AccelStepDetector` (the frozen rule), not the Android step sensors. It needs an accelerometer and a gyroscope and **no runtime permission**, so ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION is gone from the manifest.
- The Kotlin port is checked against `tune.py` on the same files (`AccelStepDetectorParityTest`, which runs only where the git-ignored CSVs exist).
- The ring screen is locked to portrait: the dev-run shake rotated it and reloaded Rin mid-mission.
